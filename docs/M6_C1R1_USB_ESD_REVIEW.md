# M6-C1R1 USB protection review

**Retain U3 pin 5 on V3V3.** ST's Figure 13 includes a3.3 V upstream transceiver; §§2.2–2.3 discuss the VCC steering reference and Figure14 shows a VCC-biased application. This review uses the rail-to-rail operating circuit and working-voltage rating to justify the installed3.3 V reference; it does not infer a requirement solely from the VBUS pin label. The installed pin mapping is 1/6 D+, 3/4 D−, 2 GND and 5 V3V3, with C10=100 nF. The pin name VBUS does not require a connection to connector VBUS in every application. [ST DS4260 Rev 7, §§2.1–2.5](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/usblc6-2.pdf).

A positive strike on a data terminal conducts through an upper steering diode to the local pin-5 rail. C10 receives some charge; the internal transil and pin-2 ground path divert surge current. A negative strike conducts through a lower steering diode from local ground. Neither direction is independent of package and PCB inductance. The internal transil is not a precision 3.3 V clamp, the buck is not credited as an ESD-current sink, and remote bulk capacitance is not credited at the nanosecond edge. Rail disturbance and residual ESP32 pad exposure require physical ESD testing. ST's own examples distinguish device clamping from extra L·di/dt voltage.

The native-geometry audit measures full-segment paths from U3.5 to C10.1 and from J2 data contacts to U3. C10's approximately 3.49 mm positive-rail path is local. The existing nearest ground stitch is approximately4.31 mm from U3.2 and1.20 mm from C10.2; filled top/bottom ground and the connector ground return remain. These distances do not establish a surge inductance. The existing local construction is retained for evaluation; its residual ESD response remains a commissioning gate. The exact final distances and via positions are recorded in [USB-ESD-review.json](../hardware/controller/review/M6-C1R1/USB-ESD-review.json). Path lengths are conservative sums of traversed segments; they are not inductance measurements. No USB data net or series resistor is changed.

| Power state | Installed topology and behavior |
|---|---|
| Battery only | Pin 5 remains biased at 3.3 V. GPIO37 senses absent VBUS; software must detach the native USB PHY. No steering connection to absent USB_VBUS is introduced. |
| USB only | TPS2553 and D3 feed the controller buck; pin 5 follows the resulting 3.3 V. PACK_PRESENT/INIT_OK gating inhibits RF. |
| Both | Existing diode-OR/current limiter remains authoritative. U3 reference remains 3.3 V. |
| Neither | Pin 5 is unpowered. A compliant host has no attached-device pull-up and does not begin transactions. |
| Host present but 3.3 V failed/decaying | Forced data signals can inject into the dead rail through steering/PHY diodes. U3 is **not** a powered-off data-isolation switch. Reset and attach sequencing must be tested; do not claim zero backfeed for an externally forced data line. |

Connecting pin 5 to actual USB_VBUS would be a different valid application in other power architectures, but offers no demonstrated improvement here. With battery-powered USB pull-up and no host, its upper steering path can charge absent VBUS and interfere with the implemented inverted VBUS detector. It also uses a higher powered steering reference. A rail-less ESD part would avoid that particular steering-rail charge-sharing path but would require a new part, footprint and residual-clamp assessment, and would not remove the MCU PHY's own unpowered injection paths. No demonstrated defect justifies that change.

The selected circuit uses the documented rail-to-rail mechanism with the existing local decoupling and return. Pin5 at3.3 V is within the5.25 V working reverse-voltage rating; that rating is not a guaranteed3.3 V surge-clamp voltage. D3/D4, D21, TPS2553, the VBUS bleeder and Q2 detection circuit are unchanged. Their existing hot reverse-leakage and USB-current qualification requirements remain; R1 does not claim them measured. USB attach must follow VBUS, not battery supply availability. Hardware reset, USB-only RF inhibition and deliberate rearming remain required.

Espressif's guidance requires appropriate ESD placement, USB series routing and correct native-USB power/connection behavior. This full-speed design does not claim 480 Mb/s operation or factory-controlled differential impedance. [ESP32-S3 hardware guidelines](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-hardware-design-guidelines/en/latest/esp32s3/schematic-checklist.html), [exact WROOM-1 module datasheet](https://www.espressif.com/sites/default/files/documentation/esp32-s3-wroom-1_wroom-1u_datasheet_en.pdf).

The charge/C10 calculations are illustrative sensitivity cases only. USB enumeration, eye quality, positive/negative IEC ESD immunity, rail overshoot, powered-off data injection and hotplug behavior are **NOT_RUN**. They are Gate D commissioning requirements, not a claimed CAD pass for IEC immunity.
