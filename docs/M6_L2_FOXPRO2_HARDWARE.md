# M6-L2 optional FoxPro2 hardware

The FoxPro option is a low-speed compatibility port, independent of USB, autonomous scheduling, RTC validity, LoRa and RF protection. All FoxPro parts are DNP in the base controller. The interface has no connection to PACK_RAW, PACK_PROT, USB VBUS or an RF supply. Its three-contact keyed JST PH connector J8 is distinct from the battery's JST XH connector. A short internal cable connects it to an insulated remotely mounted 3.5 mm TRS panel jack.

Use [DF1FO's original document](https://df1fo.darc.de/DOC/Zeitsteuerung2G.doc), pp.9,10,15, together with the plug-contact drawing and physical jack terminal sketch. Connector orientation in a generic schematic is not a jack solder-terminal numbering specification. Cross-checking the original physical jack sketch with the EBS35/KB3SPRS contact construction gives sleeve=jack terminal1, ring=terminal3, tip=terminal2; switched terminals4/5 are unused. The cable drawing in the review identifies actual plug segments; panel jack terminals must be identified by continuity with an inserted unpowered plug before wiring. Switch contacts remain unused.

| J8 contact | Cable segment | Function |
|---|---|---|
| 1, marked G | Sleeve, cable shield/return | Current-limited FoxPro ground reference |
| 2, marked D | Ring | Shared bidirectional serial data |
| 3, marked V0 | Tip | Voltage measurement **unavailable**, no battery source |

```text
Controller J8             Panel TRS jack        Straight TRS cable to FoxPro
1 G  -------------------- sleeve S  ----------- sleeve S
2 D  -------------------- ring   R  ----------- ring   R
3 V0 -------------------- tip    T  ----------- tip    T
                          switched terminals: unused
Continuity test: each S/R/T goes only to the same segment at the other end.
```

Do not connect J8 to a microphone/headphone input, a mono plug, an assumed jack pin-number scheme, or a battery charging cable. The controller does not power FoxPro; FoxPro's own battery is required. Neither the 2.4 GHz nor LoRa antenna connector is involved.

GPIO43/44 retain their recovery-UART assignments. The default population fits R300/R301 and omits R302/R303 and the complete FoxPro option. FoxPro population **omits R300/R301** and fits R302/R303. Never fit both routing sets. J3 retains its physical position and GND/TX/RX numbering. Converting back to recovery is an explicit assembly operation; software cannot select two simultaneously wired interfaces.

The transmit path is Q80, a Nexperia BSS138BK,215 MOSFET with its source permanently grounded and drain connected through the680 Ω fault limiter. SAFE supplies its gate through1 kΩ; Q81, MMBT3904LT1G, shunts that gate when GPIO43 is HIGH. Thus GPIO43 HIGH releases the bus and LOW permits a sink only when SAFE is qualified. Reset, brownout, INIT_OK low or absent pack keeps the MOS gate LOW regardless of boot-ROM/debug activity. There is no push-pull HIGH source on the bus and no NPN base-collector junction connected to it. Both transistor terminals and their actual source nets are checked by the native audit. C84,10 nF, filters drain-gate transient coupling; the calculated gate RC is about8–10 µs. SAFE only inhibits this optional output; FoxPro cannot assert SAFE or drive an RF enable.

Receive uses Q82, Nexperia BSS138BK,215, as an insulated-gate, grounded-source inverter. R309/R310 limit and divide the external input. A12V BZX84-C12 gate clamp returns positive and negative faults to GND. R311 (160 Ω) and R315 (620 Ω) define a powered HIGH above 0.75×VDD, including resistor tolerance. The 620 Ω shunt limits the unpowered GPIO to below 0.3 V even if the entire current through the 47 kΩ input limiter reaches the drain. This passive bound covers a stiff 16.8 V data fault and a 22.3 V source through the original 4.7 kΩ + 220 Ω path during ground displacement; it does not depend on assuming a low MOSFET gate leakage. The 160 Ω pull-up dissipates at most 71.4 mW with the receiver LOW, below its 82.3 mW derated 85°C rating. Budget approximately 21 mA for that receiver state. FoxPro responses require normal SAFE qualification and a present transmitter pack. USB-only or pre-initialization inspection uses native USB; the legacy port deliberately cannot transmit in those states.

Its drain has a local 3.3 V pull-up and connects to GPIO44 only through the selected link. A connected, powered FoxPro holds DATA HIGH when idle, so this inverted receiver normally draws the stated 21 mA while connected. The insulated gate avoids a base-collector injection path when the controller is unpowered. Port-side ESD diodes return to GND, not V3V3. The receiver is inverted; it uses no ordinary UART 8N1 peripheral.

The independent FoxPro supplies its original 4.7 kΩ pull-up and 220 Ω series path. ARDF-TX intentionally supplies **no active pull-up into an unpowered FoxPro**. The controller's weak data pull-down defines a disconnected condition; the decoder treats that as a held-low/incomplete bus, not a valid command. This differs from the original two-5-V-endpoint pull-up circuit and is deliberate: no 5 V rail is assumed on the cable and no controller supply powers an absent endpoint.

R304 (680 Ω, 2512 1 W) limits data fault current. R314 andR316 (each470 Ω, 2512 1 W) are parallel, providing235 Ω for the ground return while each withstands a16.8V fault within its derated power rating. Both resistors are required in the FoxPro assembly. R313 (0 Ω) bonds the unavailable voltage conductor to FoxPro ground. Its ADC is held near its own ground; no battery voltage is sourced. A tip or ground contact fault returns through the same235 Ω limiter. With a conservative0.1 Ω link envelope, a16.8 V tip/ground fault shifts the ADC reference by less than7.3mV. D80/D83/D84 are exact PESD1IVN24-AX bidirectional SOD323 devices with 24 V stand-off and a specified maximum42 V 8/20 µs clamp at3.5 A. They do not conduct appreciably during a 16.8 V DC fault. The TX MOSFET has a60 V rating. Its drain negative clamp D81 is BAS316,115 (100V, SOD323,1K/2A), whose reverse rating exceeds the42 V port-clamp envelope. RX gate series resistance/clamp and passive analog termination complete the component stress analysis. This is component-based design analysis, not a board-level IEC ESD pass.

The TX gate and shunt base have4.7 kΩ terminations. A50 µA hot-leakage sensitivity produces at most0.239 V, below the MOSFET's0.48 V minimum threshold and the unpowered GPIO injection bound. The gate HIGH remains above2.5 V in an explicit100 Ω SAFE-driver output-resistance scenario, with a100 mV driver-LOW allowance also included. [TI’s SN74LVC2G04 data](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/sn74lvc2g04.pdf) gives a3 V/16 mA/2.4 V output test point (37.5 Ω inferred resistance);100 Ω is an engineering allocation, not a guaranteed low-current I/V specification. Measure the actual SAFE/gate levels and timing in Gate G. SAFE's maximum added load is3.67 mA when the shunt is on. The680 Ω limiter bounds the MOSFET's worst16.8 V DC load-line dissipation to106 mW, below its182 mW standard-footprint85°C rating. Q81 sees only the3.3 V gate-drive circuit. An explicit20 pF drain-gate/stray allocation with9 nF minimum filtering bounds a42 V fast-step injection below0.1 V. This is a calculated design scenario; transient waveform and thermal measurements remain NOT_RUN.

The independently calculated corner/fault report reads resistor values and actual pad nets from the native board. It covers powered/unpowered endpoints, 2S/4S/USB-only/no battery, serial and analog shorts, insertion shorts, reversed/miswired conductors, ±16.8 V applied to each contact relative to controller GND, and the documented ESD envelope. It reports current, resistor dissipation, receiver-gate voltage, logic margins, leakage and limitations. Continuous multiple-source faults beyond that envelope and arbitrary external ground displacement are not a claimed operating mode. Harness insulation and the transmitter's separate pack fusing remain required.

The original receiver network is4.7k series into the ADC,10k to ground and100nF filtering; the original transmitter adds22k at the battery. Its nominal powered divider is therefore10/(22+4.7+10)=0.27248. At16.8V that predicts4.58V at the ADC, close to its5V supply and outside any guaranteed unpowered-input allowance. The original transmitter drawing specifies6–15V. Neither a nominal divider result nor a user calibration proves hot-plug, unpowered or miswire safety for a4S pack. The independently powered FoxPro ADC must not be back-powered through its input protection.

For battery reporting, both **2S and 4S are unavailable at this port**. The legacy design uses its transmitter-side 22 kΩ resistor and FoxPro ADC resistor/filter network, configured for that original supply architecture; it is not a source-backed universal 16.8 V measurement input. M6-L2 does not export raw or scaled pack voltage. An unmodified FoxPro will normally display zero; this is explicitly not pack voltage. Mark the panel **“FOXPRO — VOLTAGE N/A (2S/4S)”**. Native ARDF-TX battery identity and voltage diagnostics retain their existing accuracy and qualification limits. No falsely scaled 4S value is presented as accurate.

An unpowered FoxPro, contact insertion/withdrawal or corrupt frames cannot create an RF-enable path. Hardware fail-off, INIT_OK/MCU_EN/PACK_PRESENT qualification, independent RF power switches, battery ID, RTC backup, USB reset/recovery, both RF buffer banks and application-owned progress remain authoritative. The host protocol's test hook is not authorization to energize a PA. Physical voltage, current, leakage, cable continuity, genuine FoxPro communication, ESD and hot-plug tests remain **NOT_RUN**.
