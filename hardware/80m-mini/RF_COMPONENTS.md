# RF component and loading review

M80-E1 uses wound RF inductors rather than miniature general-purpose power chokes. The [1008LS data](https://www.coilcraft.com/en-us/products/rf/ferrite-core-chip-inductors/1008-%282520%29/1008ls/), [1812LS data](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/1df56443-b7c7-4f17-abaf-470b9bb5eee8/1812ls.pdf) and their [1008](https://www.coilcraft.com/getmedia/7d978b1b-134f-445c-9d2b-c682ece55405/spice_1008ls.pdf) / [1812](https://www.coilcraft.com/pdfs/spice_1812ls.pdf) manufacturer models are the numerical references. Model parameters are transcribed into `tools/analyze.py`; PDFs are not redistributed.

Published high-frequency Q is not substituted for operating-frequency Q. Effective RF resistance includes ferrite/model loss and can greatly exceed DCR. The 1812 model is an older manufacturer model and is conservative relative to some current Q specifications; its calculated values are not a guaranteed current-production operating-point measurement. Measure sample impedances at 3.51, 3.55 and 3.60 MHz, and repeat after assembly. Avoid interpreting DC current ratings as RF voltage ratings.

The model topology is `R2 + ((k*sqrt(f) + j*2*pi*f*L) || (R1 + 1/(j*2*pi*f*C)))`. The four LPF inductors have SRFs well above the first harmonics; layout parasitics can set a practical stopband floor. RF capacitors are C0G, 50 V. Treat the two-layer return path and fixture parasitics as part of the final filter. A low model S21 is not a guaranteed radiated harmonic result.

The 1008 lands are 1.02 × 2.54 mm at ±1.145 mm; 1812 lands are 1.14 × 3.05 mm at ±2.07 mm. These local footprints use the manufacturer recommended patterns and conservative body courtyards. The larger 1812 parts occupy antenna-bank area because 33 µH in a tiny chip would sacrifice Q/SRF. Five bare boards retain all alternatives for experiment, but the bench population omits them.

The antenna study evaluates 25/50/100/150/200 Ω resistors independently and a series-RC model for practical-wire sensitivity. At 3.55 MHz, 33 pF has approximately −1,359 Ω reactance; resonance needs roughly 61 µH before interaction with the transformer. The 2.2 µH series / 470 pF shunt section transforms a load near 100 Ω toward 50 Ω. Selecting 62.7 µH yields modeled input 57−j29 Ω for the original 100 Ω + 33 pF fixture, return loss 11.4 dB. A 100 Ω + 50 pF example with 37.7 µH gives 34−j11 Ω, 12.8 dB return loss. These are nominal examples, not a universal wire-antenna model.

`analysis/electrical-estimates.json` records currents and peak coil/capacitor voltage for a 50 Ω Thevenin source with 50 mW available. Loading voltages can be tens of volts despite a 5 V supply. Use at least a 100 V C0G capacitor for the external 33 pF artificial antenna. Published RF-coil sheets do not specify an absolute winding voltage rating: confirm suitability with the manufacturer and inspect measured stress during first-article tuning. Current headroom alone does not prove insulation margin. Reference node capacitors see much less voltage; verify that experimentally, especially with an open antenna.

A first-order thin-monopole calculation, using the short-antenna limit of equations 3.1–3.2 in this [Universität der Bundeswehr research thesis](https://athene-forschung.unibw.de/doc/89564/89564.pdf), relates the wire examples to physical lengths: radius 0.5 mm, image ground, `C = 2*pi*epsilon0*h/(ln(2*h/a)-1)` and triangular-current `Rrad = 40*pi^2*(h/lambda)^2`. At 3.55 MHz, heights 6/7.5/9 m give approximately 36.7/44.8/52.8 pF and 2.0/3.1/4.5 Ω radiation resistance. The analysis separately adds 20 Ω and 100 Ω ground-loss scenarios and searches the available loading combinations for each. It records input match, current, delivered resistive power and coil/capacitor voltage. This connects the 50 pF example to a plausible physical size without pretending to solve real soil, feed and counterpoise behavior. The approximation is less accurate toward 9 m; an impedance measurement of the installed wire takes precedence.

The same approximation gives roughly 2.7 pF and 5 mΩ radiation resistance for a 30 cm vertical. Loss and installation effects dominate. A 100 Ω + 3 pF sensitivity example gives only 0.55 dB return loss with this bank: it fails as a matched short-antenna solution. FOX10 therefore uses the separate bypass/coupling population and measured field adjustment. No high-inductance, high-Q tiny-antenna match or 30/250 m result is claimed.

The boost inductor L1 is **Sunlord SWPA3015S2R2MT**, 2.2 µH ±20%, 3 × 3 × 1.5 mm. The [manufacturer SWPA catalog](https://www.sunlordinc.com/uploads/files/20221122/SWPA%20series%20of%20SMD%20Power%20Inductor.pdf) and manufacturer series specification establish the package; distributor descriptions sometimes conflate heat and saturation current. The detailed 2023 series sheet gives 1.6 A minimum saturation / 2.0 A typical for the 2.2 µH family, above the TPS61099 maximum approximately 1.25 A current limit. Confirm the exact MT approval sheet before assembly. It is a power-converter component, not an RF-coil substitute. Its approximate 78 mΩ maximum DCR, tolerance and minimum saturation are the conservative design basis.

C4's 22 µF / 10 V X5R part is derated for DC bias. [TI's capacitor example](https://www.ti.com/lit/an/sluaad3/sluaad3.pdf) reports about 50% loss at 5 V for GRM21BR61A226ME44. Applying the −20% initial tolerance gives about 8.8 µF before additional temperature/aging allowances. Confirm effective capacitance and boost loop stability/startup at the actual bias and temperature; the 8 µF design target has limited margin. There is no claim of a measured startup or ripple result.
